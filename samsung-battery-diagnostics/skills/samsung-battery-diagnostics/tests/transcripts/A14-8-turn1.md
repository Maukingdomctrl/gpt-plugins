Good question to ask before spending money. The short answer: **age alone isn't enough evidence to say the battery is failing or needs replacing.** At almost 3 years, some wear is plausible, but drain can just as easily come from apps, signal or settings, and replacing the battery wouldn't fix those. Let's check with evidence first.

**What I have so far**
- **Observed:** your Galaxy A14 5G is almost 3 years old.
- I can't see your phone and don't have any measurements yet, so I can't say whether the battery is worn.

**First, a safety check:** if you notice **swelling** (screen or back lifting), a **burning/chemical smell**, the phone getting **too hot to hold**, or liquid damage, stop charging and using it and contact an authorised Samsung service centre. That overrides everything below.

Otherwise, please send:

1. **Model number:** Settings > About phone > **Model number** (should start with **SM-A146**, e.g. SM-A146B or SM-A146P). The Galaxy A14 4G (SM-A145…) is a different phone I don't cover.
2. **Software:** Settings > About phone > Software information: **One UI Core version** and **Android version**.
3. **Samsung's own battery check:** Samsung Members > **Support** > Phone diagnostics > Get started > **Battery status**. It reports **Normal**, **Weak** or **Bad**. Please tell me which (a screenshot is fine).
4. **Shutdowns:** has the phone ever switched off while still showing charge left? If so, at about what %, and was it cold?
5. **% behaviour:** does the battery % ever **jump** suddenly (e.g. 30 % to 5 %) or get stuck?
6. **What made you suspect it:** how long a full charge lasts now vs. before, and whether that changed gradually or suddenly (e.g. after an update or new app).
7. **One overnight measurement** (no charging): bedtime % and time, wake-up % and time.

If Samsung Members says Weak or Bad, or the phone shuts down with charge left, replacement becomes a reasonable discussion (at an authorised service centre). If it says Normal and nothing else points to wear, we'll look for a software or usage cause instead, which is usually cheaper to fix.
